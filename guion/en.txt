Croatoan. A single word, carved into a post.
Three years earlier, John White left more than a hundred colonists on Roanoke. Among them, his daughter and his newborn granddaughter.
He sailed to England for supplies. The war with Spain delayed his return.
In 1590, when he finally returns, the village is empty.
No bodies. No sign of a fight. The houses taken down.
They had agreed on a signal: if they left in danger, they would carve a cross.
There was no cross. Only that word.
Croatoan: the name of a nearby island, and of its people.
A storm kept him from searching. White never saw his family again.
Did they leave? Were they attacked? Did they join another tribe?
More than four centuries later, no one knows.
